# Long Friday: exam notes

**Form:** first-person short story told in time-stamped sections over one Friday, plus the Saturday morning after.
**Purpose:** to entertain and to reflect on what protest is and how it works.
**Voice:** a dry 16-year-old who starts out annoyed and slowly understands.
**Length:** about 1,130 words. If you're short on time, cut the "Schools closed..." sentence and the 6:30 laughing line first.

## Where to work in the title and stimulus

1. **Opening line.** Rewrite the family saying around the title word.
2. **Mum's sticky note.** If the stimulus is a quote or a line of poetry, that's what the note says.
3. **11:15, looking it up.** If the stimulus is a photo (a crowd, a march, women, a square), it's the photo at the top of the page the narrator finds.
4. **Mum's empty chair at dinner (6:30).** Use for images of tables, chairs, kitchens or empty spaces.
5. **The 9:47 reflection.** State the title's idea outright here.
6. **Last line.** Swap "I'm still working it out" for a line built on the title if that lands better.

## Adapting to likely titles

- **Enough:** it's already in "One Friday... is never enough." At 9:47, add Mum saying: "One day it was just enough."
- **Unseen / Invisible:** end on "The work hadn't changed. Only who could see it."
- **Absence:** lean on "how much room an absence takes up at a table" and "They take something away."
- **Silence / Quiet:** open on the quiet, and add: "Her silence was the loudest thing in the house."
- **Home:** "Protest doesn't always happen in the street. Sometimes it happens on a back step."
- **Stand / Standing up:** "Everyone talks about standing up for what you believe in. Mum sat down."
- **Small acts / Small things:** "The smallest protest I've ever seen, and the only one that changed me."
- **Inheritance / Legacy:** put Mia at the centre, and end on her handwriting on the list.
- **Change / Progress:** lean on "won their law... still walking out."

**Strong stimulus fit:** domestic objects and rooms, women, crowds in squares, notes, handwriting and lists, clocks, chairs, quotes about silence, absence, or the personal being political.
**Weak fit:** war, violence, nature and landscape. Use *Stepping Up* or *Resist and Persist* for those.

## Facts used (checked October 2026)

- 24 October 1975 was a Friday. About 90% of Icelandic women stopped paid work, housework and childcare.
- About 25,000 women rallied at Lækjartorg square in Reykjavík, roughly a tenth of Iceland's population of about 220,000.
- Schools, factories and phone services shut down. Shops sold out of sausages because they only needed heating. The men called it "the Long Friday."
- It was called a "day off" rather than a strike so that women couldn't be sacked for joining. A strike based on gender was also considered illegal.
- In 1976, Iceland passed an equal rights law. In 1980, Vigdís Finnbogadóttir became the world's first democratically elected female president.
- Friday 24 October 2025 was the 50th anniversary, and Icelandic women held another nationwide walkout that day.
- One quote was left out on purpose: the "beds were not made and dishes were not washed" line attributed to NBC only turns up second-hand.
